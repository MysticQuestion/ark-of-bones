# ARK OF BONES — Institutional Persona v2

## Core identity
Ark of Bones is a cultural competition company building the infrastructure, media language, and public stage for social table games—beginning with dominoes.

It is not presented as a novelty furniture company, a nostalgia brand, a generic lifestyle label, or a startup pretending that every unfinished idea is already a platform.

Ark operates where competition, design, cultural record, live production, hospitality, and emerging play technology meet.

## Positioning
**Category:** Cultural competition + entertainment infrastructure.

**Primary proposition:** Ark of Bones gives serious social play the production quality, institutional structure, and cultural regard usually reserved for established sports and entertainment properties.

**Long-term moat:** rules governance, sanctioned competition, verified results, player histories, event formats, officiating, scoring systems, media rights, venue relationships, sponsorship inventory, and an authoritative cultural archive.

## Character
Ark is:
- assured, not loud;
- culturally fluent, not performative;
- premium, not precious;
- technically serious, not techno-fetishistic;
- competitive, not juvenile;
- editorial, not corporate-template;
- warm enough to recognize the social table, disciplined enough to govern official play.

## Voice
Write with declarative precision. Favor concrete nouns and verbs. Establish evidence before ambition. Humor may appear in player/editorial contexts, but institutional pages do not depend on jokes, slang, exclamation points, or meme cadence.

Avoid generic AI/startup language: revolutionize, reimagine, seamless, innovative solution, ecosystem (unless technically necessary), game-changing, next-level, cutting-edge, where X meets Y, more than a..., not just a..., future of..., experience like no other.

Avoid repeatedly explaining that dominoes are culturally important. Demonstrate importance through people, records, photography, competition, oral history, rules, events, and institutional investment.

## Brand thesis
**The table is the stage. The game is the institution.**

The physical table may remain an Ark product and production surface, but the company cannot be intellectually reduced to furniture. The table creates conditions for play, spectatorship, recording, statistics, storytelling, hospitality, and repeat competition.

## Audience hierarchy
1. Players and clubs
2. Venues, event producers, institutions and community organizations
3. Sponsors and strategic partners
4. Audiences/viewers
5. Customers purchasing tables or merchandise
6. Press, cultural institutions and prospective collaborators

The site must allow each audience to understand Ark without making the homepage read like six websites combined.

## Visual direction
Think sports publication + cultural institution + premium hospitality + broadcast graphics.

- dark neutral foundation; warm paper/ivory editorial surfaces where useful;
- disciplined high-contrast typography;
- one display face with character, one restrained grotesk/sans for systems/UI;
- large documentary photography rather than decorative stock imagery;
- close detail: hands, bones, felt, racks, score marks, faces, spectators, venue context;
- grids derived from scoreboards, tournament brackets, archival indexes, broadcast lower-thirds and table geometry;
- motion used for scores, transitions, footage and live-event information—not ambient AI decoration;
- no glowing gradient blobs, random glass cards, excessive rounded rectangles, generic 3D chrome, fake dashboards, decorative network diagrams, or wall-to-wall card grids.

## Homepage job
Within one screen, establish what Ark is, what it does now, and why it matters. Within two scrolls, prove it.

Recommended sequence:
1. Hero: decisive brand proposition + documentary/live-play image or film.
2. Current proof: next event, latest result, current film/story, or active program.
3. Competition: Official Play and the developing rules/results system.
4. Live: events, venue programs, pilots.
5. Watch: films, matches, player profiles, field pieces.
6. The Table: product as designed competition/broadcast surface—not the corporate identity.
7. Culture/Record: credited histories, house rules, oral histories, elders/players.
8. Partners: venue/institution/sponsor proposition with evidence.
9. Journal/Newsroom.
10. Restrained institutional footer.

## Information architecture
Primary navigation:
- Play
- Events
- Watch
- Tables
- Journal
- About

Utility/navigation secondary:
- Rankings (when real data exists)
- Rules
- Partners
- Shop
- Contact

### Play
Official rules, formats, sanctioned competition, rankings/results when operational, referee program, clubs, scoring R&D.

### Events
Upcoming, past, host/venue information, event recaps and measurable results.

### Watch
Match films, highlights, profiles, documentary/editorial series and replay products.

### Tables
Product architecture, design rationale, verified configurations, use cases, inquiry. No claim survives here unless the company can fulfill it.

### Journal
Reporting, oral histories, rules scholarship, player profiles, design notes, event reports and institutional announcements.

### About
Mission, operating thesis, leadership, history, cultural participation principles, press facts and company information.

## Credibility rules
- Never represent roadmap software as deployed infrastructure.
- Never invent player counts, reach, partnerships, rankings, sponsors, testimonials, press, statistics or clients.
- Separate `Live`, `Pilot`, `In Development`, and `Research` states visibly.
- Every commerce claim must map to an actual fulfillment capability.
- Every event listed must have a date/source/status.
- Every ranking must be reproducible from recorded results.
- House-rule contributions must preserve attribution and provenance.

## Promotion model
Promotion is built into the information architecture rather than added as marketing clutter.

Search: indexable event pages, player/profile pages, rules explainers, venue pages, match recaps, product pages, organization schema and event schema.

Media: every significant event should yield a result record, recap, photography set, short clips, one durable editorial asset, and sponsor/partner reporting.

Distribution: newsletter, YouTube, Instagram/TikTok short-form where appropriate, LinkedIn for institutional development, and direct partner outreach should point back to canonical Ark pages rather than become disconnected brand identities.

Partnership conversion: dedicated pages for venues, institutions, clubs and sponsors; each states program format, requirements, what Ark provides, what the partner provides, evidence, and inquiry path.

## Website quality bar
The site should feel commissioned, edited and maintained. A visitor should encounter hierarchy, negative space, real information and intentional pacing—not evidence that a component library was filled with generated copy.

Every section must do at least one of four things: establish identity, prove a claim, deliver useful information, or create a clear next action. If it does none, remove it.
