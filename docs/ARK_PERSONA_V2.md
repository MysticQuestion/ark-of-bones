# ARK OF BONES — Persona v2

## Editorial rule
Ark does not sell itself with explanation-heavy copy.

The site should communicate through image, scale, structure, records, names, dates, scores, products, events, and documented activity.

Words are used only when they add information.

## Voice
Short. Exact. Controlled.

Prefer:
- names
- dates
- scores
- locations
- specifications
- rules
- status
- credits
- direct actions

Avoid:
- pitch language
- startup language
- brand manifestos
- marketing superlatives
- artificial urgency
- stacked slogans
- rhetorical questions
- repeated mission statements
- “not X, but Y” constructions
- “more than” constructions
- “where X meets Y” constructions
- “X + Y = Z” constructions
- “the future of” constructions
- inflated cultural claims
- copy written only to fill space

Do not use phrases such as:
- reimagine
- revolutionize
- elevate
- transform
- redefine
- next-level
- cutting-edge
- seamless
- immersive experience
- game-changing
- built for the future
- more than a game
- more than a brand
- not just
- at the intersection of

## Character
Ark is disciplined, serious, culturally fluent, competitive, and visually confident.

That character should be inferred from the site rather than repeatedly described by the site.

## Core public language
Use `ARK OF BONES` prominently.

Secondary language should remain functional:
- Events
- Official Play
- Watch
- Tables
- Shop
- Journal
- About
- Rules
- Rankings
- Partners
- Contact

## Homepage
The homepage does not need a paragraph explaining Ark.

Preferred opening patterns:

### Option A
`ARK OF BONES`

Full-bleed image or film.

Then current information:
`NEXT EVENT`
`DATE`
`CITY`
`REGISTER`

### Option B
`ARK OF BONES`
`DOMINOES`

Image or film.

### Option C
Logo only over strong live-play footage.

Navigation and current event information provide context.

No explanatory subheadline is required if the visual and information hierarchy already establish the subject.

## Page-copy standard
Every text block must answer at least one concrete question:
- What is this?
- Who is involved?
- When is it happening?
- Where is it happening?
- What happened?
- What are the rules?
- What is available?
- What does it cost?
- What is the status?
- What action can the visitor take?

If a block answers none of these, remove it.

## Visual direction
Use documentary photography, live footage, tournament data, scoreboards, brackets, product detail, archival material, and restrained typography.

Avoid decorative UI made to compensate for weak content.

No generic card walls.
No gradient spectacle.
No ornamental dashboards.
No fake statistics.
No stock-business imagery.
No decorative copy blocks.

## Credibility
- Do not state a capability that does not exist.
- Do not publish roadmap work as finished work.
- Do not invent reach, partners, rankings, testimonials, attendance, press, or results.
- Commerce language must match actual fulfillment.
- Event information must be current and sourced.
- Rankings must come from recorded results.
- Rules must be versioned.
- Cultural or historical material must be credited.

## Status language
Where development status matters, use only:
- Live
- Pilot
- In Development
- Research

Do not wrap these labels in promotional explanation.

## Final standard
The site should feel edited.

When an image, score, date, name, table, bracket, caption, or piece of footage can communicate the point better than a paragraph, use the image, score, date, name, table, bracket, caption, or footage.
